REPORT: LANDING RETROSPECTIVE

Subject: Demographic Attrition, Occupational Misalignment, and Social Friction
Target Reading Status: Complete after reading Book 1

SPOILER NOTICE: This document analyzes the systemic factors that led to initial colonial instability following landing. Ensure you have completed Book 1 before proceeding.

1. Flight Crew Attrition & Agoraphobia

Certain factors contributed significantly to the difficulties encountered by the colony after landing.

The advanced age of the last generation of flight crew, combined with the extreme physical stress of landing and the total change of lifestyle, led to a high death rate among them shortly after arrival. Furthermore, widespread agoraphobia among the surviving crew meant that even those who lived longer seldom left the ship to visit the colony core.

2. Fertility Protocols & Unplanned Demographic Strain

The cohort ages of 16, 18, 20, and 22 at landing were specifically picked to provide a capable workforce that was adaptable to the new planet. They were gestated in artificial wombs like the flight crew before them, but they were born fertile.

The flight crew had all been born sterile, as interstellar radiation during the transit rendered natural procreation non-viable. Being able to have children naturally on the ground was considered a critical safety protocol for the long-term survival of the species.

However, education regarding fertility control was missed. Unexpected and unplanned pregnancies placed severe additional stress on the young, developing colony.

3. Occupational Assignment & Labor Misalignment

The landing generation had been tested for aptitudes and assigned their final job roles at eight years of age, following the exact protocol used for crew generations during flight. This allowed their entire education to be tailored to their final roles. They were taught that all jobs were essential to the survival of the colony.

Because of the massive population increase from the flight crew to the landing generation, many new job roles were added that the original flight crew were not familiar with. That meant those children did not receive the same reinforcement at home that their jobs were essential. Unfortunately, many of these new specialized roles were not needed immediately upon landing, but were required only after the original infrastructure build-out.

As a result, a large portion of the landing generation were assigned to general labor gangs to execute heavy construction work that had little to do with their actual training.

4. Social Resentment & The Ruin Catalyst

This mismatch led to widespread resentment among the landing generation. The original aptitude tests were written by people long dead back in Sol, and the realities of the new world were so fundamentally different from life aboard the ship that the youth did not want to remain bound by those ancient test parameters, especially when their assigned roles appeared non-essential on the ground.

When the ruins were discovered, they offered an immediate and irresistible escape from that rigidly defined future.

5. Command Reaction

Command issued the land allotments early in an attempt to hold on to the remaining workforce. They also released footage from within the structure along with educational modules that taught equivalent crafts using Earth methods.

Since the colony core build was incomplete, advanced materials for the construction of private residences and farms were unavailable. The landing generation built their new homes with the materials that were available, wood, stone and brick. They embraced a lower technology, craft oriented lifestyle in reaction to their technologically advanced but tightly controlled childhoods.

Recommendations

Although no one could have predicted the presence of the ruins and structure, the following recommendations may still benefit another colony.

The spacing of generations should be altered to allow the last generation of flight crew to be in their mid-forties on landing.

The last generation of flight crew, and the landing generation should be thoroughly educated on fertility control and pregnancy.

More flexibility in the job assignments for the landing generation should be allowed. It needs to be clearly explained that some jobs are not needed at landing, but will become essential in the long term.

End of Report
