THE USER MANUAL

Subject: Magical Crafting in the Structure

Prerequisite: Read through Book 3 before opening these notes.

1. Overview

Crafting in the game is controlled by the pattern map. Each beginning craft has an entry level pattern that allows a new Player to begin crafting. The pattern map is actually a graph, and all the patterns eventually connect together. When a beginning fiber crafter opens the pattern map, it will be centered on the pattern to make a crafting bag. The pattern is open for everyone, but will appear faded until it is mastered. The surrounding patterns will be dark. When a crafter masters a pattern, the pattern solidifies. Learning a pattern also gives the crafter the chance to open connecting patterns. Patterns are more likely to be revealed if the crafter has:

1. Worked with all the materials before.

2. Knows all the magic spells needed.

3. Has seen the completed item.

4. Has inspected the item.

5. Has repaired the item.

The pattern graph is reevaluated each time a crafter masters a pattern, learns a new spell or works with a new material. Patterns can open as far as three connections away from a newly mastered pattern, although that is rare.

Note: Although creating an item isn't recognized as a trigger point for the pattern map to be reevaluated, it might be, since a pattern is guaranteed to open if the crafter manages to make the item without a pattern, or by altering another pattern. -E. T.

WARNING:
The Tier level given for crafts in this document are human created. They are ranked here in the order they were discovered and by complexity.

2. Tier 0: Non-Crafts

There are many occupations in the structure that are not considered a craft because there isn't a pattern map associated with them. Tools and magic are still used in these occupations. The finished products are not integrated. They can not be put into inventory. Since they are identical outside of the Game, they can be traded with the colony core. Butchering, Cooking and Brewing are examples.

3. Tier 1: Base Crafts

The following crafts are considered the starter or basic crafts in the game.

A. Leather

Although skinning and butchering are considered non-crafts, tanning leather using integrated tools results in an integrated product. Leather can be made thinner during the tanning process but not thicker. Cutting leather with a knife or sheet cutter, opens the leather crafting pattern map.

B. Fiber

Thread can be spun from plant and animal fibers and fiber scrap and debris using a spindle. A loom turns thread fabric. The type of weave is controlled by spells. Fabric is cut using a set of integrated shears, which opened the fiber crafting pattern map.

C. Wood

Wood planks can be made from felled trees or dead fall, or from wood scrap. The woodworking pattern map is accessed through the crosscut saw.

D. Metal

Metal ingots can be made from scrap, debris or ore. The metalworking pattern map is accessed through the blacksmith's anvil. The below table is a sample of the many metals that can be crafted. The listed tier it the tier of the spell needed to work the metal.

Tier Metal
0 Black Iron
1 Steel
2 Bronze
3 Copper
4 Aluminum

*Note: These listings give the most common tool used to access the pattern map for each craft. Once opened using these starter tools, it can be accessed using higher tier tools. --E. T.

3. Tier 2: Advanced Crafts

The following crafts are considered advanced or tier 2 crafts in the game.

A. Glass

Glass scrap exists but is rare. There is both glass debris and recyclables. Glass can be created from silicon sand. The glass pattern map is tied to the blowing tube.

B. Stone

There is no known stone scrap. There is both stone debris and recyclables, but most stone is rock, collected from the surface or, industrial area and mines. The green spaces inside the structure contain very little rock. The stone pattern map is tied to the chisel.

C. Clay

There is no known clay scrap. There are no know recycling spells. Since clay is fired, pottery debris can not be converted back to clay. Clay can be collected from the surface and is the industrial area and mines. It is a rare find in ruined shops. The clay pattern map is tied to the spinning wheel.

D. Bone

Bone crafting has expanded to include all organic components that are not leather. This includes bone, claws, tusks and shell. The same knife, saw and drill that is used in other crafts, will reveal the bone pattern map when working on bone.

*Note: Since the same pattern is used for bone, tusk, shell, wood and metal buttons this functionality was not noticed for some time. --E. T.

3. Tier 3: Compound Crafts

The following crafts are considered compound or tier 3 crafts in the game. They combine multiple of the singular craft described above. If trained by a master, an apprentice can open a pattern in the center of the crafting group.

A. Jewelery

Jewelery combines fiber, metal, stone and glass crafting.

B. Furniture

Furniture combines wood, metal, glass, clay and fiber crafting.

4. Tier and Quality

The tier of a finished item is a measure of how well it works. The quality is a measure of how long it lasts. Both attributes are dependent on the tier of the materials, tools and spells used, and the tier of the crafter. Material tier is not fixed but varies with the pattern used.

All integrated products can be repaired. Repairing returns an items condition to 100%, but it reduces the durability of the item, making the item wear out quicker. Durability is impacted less by many small repairs than one large repair.

5. Crafter Tiers and Titles

Crafters organize themselves into workshops working under a master. This culture has lead to a set of titles associated with tier. Crafting is determined by the highest spell they know in a craft. Usually this means their tier matches their main craft, but they are lower tier crafters in secondaries.
