THE USER MANUAL

Subject: Controlling Structure Technology

Prerequisite: Read through Book 1 before opening these notes.

1. General Interface Philosophy

Whoever built the Structure designed their UI to be intuitive. It includes no language, is controlled by natural gestures and features simplified historical pictograms. There are symbols in the structure. The symbols around the prize altars and vendors appear to have no meaning and are purely decoration. The only symbols with meaning are used to indicate numbers. The symbols have a value of zero through six in seven different fonts, giving 49 different symbols. See the entry on numbers to learn how higher values than six are written.

*Note: Unfortunately their definition of natural gestures was based on very different biology. Their historical version of technology does not match the human versions. This means that many of their pictograms are completely indecipherable to the colonists from Sol. --I. W.

2. Gesture Controls

Much of the technology embedded in the structure is controlled with gestures. Controls are initiated with a start signal or symbol and completed with an end symbol. Between the control number or numbers of the desired technology is signaled.

*Note: Companion has only two "fingers". This limits the amount of magic he can cast even more than the five finger limit we humans are dealing with. He replaces signalling with his hands with singing notes. I believe this is an accessibility feature and one of the original builder races must also have had a limited number of fingers. Although this adaptation does not work everywhere. --I. W.

A. Finger groupings

Much of the offensive and defensive technology is control with the use of finger groupings. Colonists that settled in the structure call this wizard magic.

Start Symbol: The user holds their hand up with all fingers spread and then closes it down into a fist.
0 Symbol: The closed fist.
1 Symbol: The open hand, held in front of the user, with all their fingers together and their thumb tight against the side of their hand.
2 Symbol: The open hand, held in front of the user, with fingers held in two groupings. The most common is all fingers together and the thumb held out from the hand.
3 Symbol: The open hand, held in front of the user, with fingers held in three groupings. The most common is index, middle and ring fingers together with the thumb and pinky held out from the hand.
4 Symbol: The open hand, held in front of the user, with fingers held in four groupings. The most common is index, middle together with all other fingers spread.
5 Symbol: The open hand with all fingers spread.
6 Symbol: Unknown.
End Symbol: There are many end symbols. The most known is the throw. This is an empty throw gesture with the fingers of the hand held in the last number symbol configuration.

*Note: The problem of not being able to signal six with only five fingers limits the number of controls that can be signalled. Humans are unable to use any control that contains a six, and are barred entirely from the six tree control set. --I. W.

Example: Tier 0 light This control is used to turn the light panels on the ceiling on.

1. Look at light panel.

2. Start Symbol: Raise hand, spread fingers wide, make fist.

3. Number 0: Loosen fist slightly, form fist again.

4. End Symbol: Make a throw gesture with fist. The AR overlay will render a glowing white sphere that appears just beyond the user's hand at the end of the throw. The ball of light will impact the light panel exactly where the user was looking. The light panel will switch on.

CRITICAL WARNING ON GESTURE REGISTRATION:
When using a throwing motion, the target is not where the user is aiming at, but where they are looking. Do not look at an ally when signalling for an attack.

B. Squeezed

Squeezes are used to turn on a function in a hand held item. This includes adding an element to a weapon. Colonists that settled in the structure call this warrior magic.

Start Symbol: Tighten their hold on the item one finger at a time, starting with the pinky and ending with the thumb. This is followed by a squeeze with all fingers.
0 Symbol: A squeeze with all fingers.
1 Symbol: Tighten grip using thumb alone.
2 Symbol: Tighten grip on index finger, followed by tightening thumb.
3 Symbol: Tighten grip on middle finger, then index finger, then thumb.
4 Symbol: Tighten grip on ring finger, middle finger, then index finger, then thumb.
5 Symbol: Tighten grip one finger at a time, starting with pinky and working down to thumb.
6 Symbol: Unknown.
End Symbol: There are many end symbols. This signalling technique is most commonly used to imbue a weapon with an element. The end symbol for that is to make the strike.

Example: Tier 0 light blade This control is used to imbue light into a weapon, which makes it glow.

1. Hold integrated weapon in a vertical position.

2. Start Symbol: Tighten hold one finger at a time from pinky to thumb, squeeze with all fingers, release to normal hold.

3. Number 0: Squeeze with all fingers.

4. End Symbol: Twist weapon. Weapon will glow, illuminating surroundings. The spell will cancel if the weapon is tilted more than ten degrees off vertical.

WARNING ON LIGHT BLADE SPELL:
Light Blade only works on integrated weapons, or weapons manufactured using structure technology or found within. Most imbuing spells will work on any weapon, including knives brought in from the colony manufactory. It will even work on sticks if they are used as a weapon.

C. Tapped

Taps are used to control function in tools and appliances. Since this includes the tools used for manufacturing, colonists that settled in the structure call this crafter magic.

Start Symbol: Tap one finger at a time, starting with the pinky and ending with the thumb, on the surface of the tool. Follow by tapping the tool with the palm of the hand.
0 Symbol: Tap the tool with the palm of the hand.
1 Symbol: Tap the tool with the thumb.
2 Symbol: Tap the tool with the index finger, followed by the thumb.
3 Symbol: Tap the tool with the middle finger, then index finger, then thumb.
4 Symbol: Tap the tool with the ring finger, middle finger, index finger, then thumb.
5 Symbol: Tap the tool with one finger at a time, starting with pinky and working down to thumb.
6 Symbol: Unknown.
End Symbol: There are many end symbols. One end symbol it to tap all fingers at once.

Example: Tier 0 burn stick This control is set a stick on fire. It is very useful for starting campfires.

1. Set stick on ground.

2. Start Symbol: Tap one finger at a time from pinky to thumb, touch stick palm of hand.

3. Number 0: Tap one finger at a time from pinky to thumb.

4. End Symbol: Tap all fingers together, stick will ignite.

*Note: Utility spells, like changing the temperature of the water in a sink, do not require a start or stop symbol. They also don't include a learning period. To make the water hotter tap five on the edge of the sink while the water is running. To make it colder, tap three. --I. W.

3. Written

Control functionality can also be signaled with written numbers. This method is called enchanting.

Enchanting is used to add new properties to items. The font, size, number and distribution of the symbols over the surface of the item to be enchanted all affect the strength, functionality and lifetime of the enchantment.

Start Symbol: Starting with 6, write the numbers 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 in a counter-clockwise circle. Write zero in the center. All numbers must be written in the same structure font
0-6 Symbols: These are the same symbols used for each number in the start symbol.
End Symbol: To finish the enchantment the start symbol is erased. The symbols of the enchantment will sink into the substrate leaving only a slight water look to the surface to indicate it is enchanted.

*Note: In enchantment the 6 symbol is finally accessible for humans. Since the 6 tree is mass or momentum, this allows enchantments to reduce the weight of a bag, or increase the speed of an arrow. --I. W.

4. Map

A virtual map is available to all users. The map is of the structure only. It does not include surface topography. It only includes area the user has already visited. It includes all area around the person that could have been seen, even if they didn't look in that direction. It does not include anything that can't be seen, such as area behind closed doors.

Opening: Hold one hand palm up, with fingers parrallel to their body. Lay the other hand on top, palm down, with fingers pointing away from the body, or 90 degrees to the first hand. Rotate the second hand, using the pinky as a hinge point until the second hand is also palm up. Once open the second hand can be dropped. Right and left hands are interchangeable.
Navigation: The map contains a symbol that resembles an X with an L wrapped around it. This is used for navigation. The bottom line of the L is east-west. The side line of the L is south-north. The X lines of the X zooms in and out and up and down levels.
Closing: Moving suddenly or dropping both hands closes the map.

*Note: The map is drawn with South at the top --I. W.

4. Individual

Each person in the game has a personal interface. This is the hardest of all interfaces to decipher. Most of its functionality is unknown. It can be used to decipher the wall inscriptions found in the structure. The font it appears in changes over time. This change in font has been defined as a change in the person's tier, as it does correlate with a users mastery of more complex controls.

Opening: Hold eyes completely steady. A blinking light will appear at the edge of the users vision. Usher this light to the point the user is looking out using hand motions. Do not move eyes. When the point of light is dead center, it will expand out into the personal interface.
Use: The interface is a mix of structure numbers and icons. The icons can be selected. Some open additional tools. Others turn solid or transparent, which defines a choice or setting. Once open some movement of the user's eyes is allowed. See Closing.
Closing: Moving suddenly closes the interface.

5. Physical

There are physical interfaces also built into the game. The interfaces are tied to a physical item. Touching the item opens the interface. The interface appears on the linked item. The interfaces are composed of numbers and icons. Icons and numbers can be selected and dragged using touch. Stepping away from the item closes the interface.

A. Prize Altar

This interface item spawns in random rooms in the structure. It appears as a stone shelf with a smooth flat panel of stone directly above it. It is on this panel that the interface appears. Symbols are carved into the wall around the flat panel. They are decoration only. There is one in every housing unit.

The Prize Altar function is to put item in and out of inventory. Only integrated items can be place into inventory. It can also be used to add and remove coins from the user's account and check the current balance.

B. Vendor

This interface item spawns in select rooms in the structure. It appears as a stone shelf with a smooth flat panel of stone directly above it. It is on this panel that the interface appears. Symbols are carved into the wall around the flat panel. They are decoration only. They are a different set than the ones on prize altars. There is one in every housing square in the market area, and a food only one in the kitchen of the inn. They can appear in ruined shops and galleries.

A user can sell items they are carrying to the vendor, or purchase items from the vendor from a list. The list of available items vary with each vendor. The vendors with the largest available inventory are the ones that appear in the market areas of squares.

Most bought items appear on the shelf. Rarely, in market square's only, bought items appear in front of the Vendor on the ground. All items sold to the vendor must be placed on the shelf. When they disappear the purchase price is added to the user's account. A vendor incorporates a pay surface for payments. It can be used to check a user's balance. It can not be used to add or pull coins directly to a users account.

C. Shop Interface

Every shop in a square has a service counter. The shop's interface is built into the counter.

The shop interface includes a pay surface. The pay surface will accept physical coins or coins from a customer's account. A customer's account balance can not be viewed.

D. Market Pay Surfaces

The market area of a square includes a set of small pillars. The top of these pillars are pay surfaces where a customer can pay a seller in physical coins or from their account, see Shadow Coin Payment below. There are no markings on these Pillars. Payment can be made with physical coins by setting the coins on the pay surface. When enough coins are present to cover the price, the paid coins will disappear, to be replaced by any change owed.

*Note: Physical coins are not accepted until enough coins are present to cover the price. The coins then disappear, to be replaced by any change owed. --I. W.

E. Shadow Coin Payment

Payment for rent on a room, shop or secured storage is done via shadow coins. Shadow coins are a set of translucent coins that appear on doors or surfaces. The number and type of coin that appear match the amount owed. A user makes a gesture like they are pulling coins from a purse or bag on their belt. The amount is deducted from their account.
